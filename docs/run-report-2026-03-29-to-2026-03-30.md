## Executive Summary

The bot ran stably for about 23.5 hours and did open trades, but only in the first 42 minutes of the run.

High-level outcome:
- `20` directional paper trades were opened
- all `20` were weather trades
- `0` trades from this run were closed
- `0` outcomes were recorded
- `0` realized P&L was produced
- the bot spent the rest of the run scanning markets but not entering new positions because the open-position capacity filled and never drained

Deployment conclusion:
- Do **not** deploy real money yet
- entry logic is functioning
- lifecycle turnover is not proven
- this run does not show that fast-resolving markets move from `open` to `resolved` to `closed`

Top risk-block reasons:

This means the bot spent most of the run in a saturated state.

### 5. The March 29 weather positions never closed

This is the critical failure for deployment readiness.

The `20` weather positions opened during this run were still `status=open` in the latest deduped state:
- `resolved_at = null`
- no close events seen in `main.log`
- no resolution events seen in `trading.resolution.log`
- no move to pending redeem
- no realized outcome in `fills.jsonl`

The resolution log only showed heartbeats such as:
- `resolution heartbeat: tracked_open=20 pending_redeem=0`

That means the lifecycle loop was alive, but it did not convert these positions into resolved/closed state during the observed run.

## Why Trades Were Entered

From `fills.jsonl`, the weather entries were driven mainly by:
- `weather_forecast_confidence`
- sometimes `weather_forecast_agreement`

Trade logic pattern:
- `BUY_YES` when model probability materially exceeded market price
- `BUY_NO` when market YES price looked too high relative to the model

Examples:
- NYC `<=47°F`: market `0.04`, model `0.8886`, EV `0.8346`
- Tel Aviv `<=21°C`: market `0.0005`, model `0.8214`, EV `0.8154`
- Seoul `16°C`: NO-side weather short with market YES `0.9645`, model YES `0.1504`, EV `0.8046`

So the entry engine did exactly what it was supposed to do: find cheap or overpriced weather buckets and take the modeled side.

## What the Run Actually Proved

This run proves:
- the bot can stay up for about a day
- feeds and market monitoring are stable enough for a long run
- the strategy can discover many markets
- the directional weather entry logic is functioning
- the EV/risk pipeline can select and place trades

This run does **not** prove:
- that trades resolve correctly
- that tracked positions drain after market resolution
- that outcomes are recorded to `fills.jsonl`
- that realized P&L is trustworthy
- that open inventory can turn over fast enough for continuous deployment
- that the bot is ready for real money

## Deployment Readiness

Current verdict: **not ready for real money**

Reason:
- you have evidence of entry generation
- you do not have evidence of lifecycle completion

The most important missing proof is:
- `open -> resolved -> closed` on a fast-resolving category

Weather was supposed to be that proof lane, but this run showed the opposite:
- weather positions opened fast
- weather positions stayed open
- no outcomes were written

## What Must Happen Before Real Money

1. Run another paper session where fast-resolving weather markets actually close automatically.
2. Confirm `fills.jsonl` gets non-null `outcome` and `resolved_at`.
3. Confirm `positions.jsonl` open count drains after resolution.
4. Confirm realized P&L moves away from zero based on actual outcomes.
5. Confirm the bot can free slots naturally and continue opening new positions without manual intervention.

## Most Important Takeaway

The entry side of the bot is working.

The lifecycle side is still not proven in practice.

Until a full fast-resolution paper cycle completes automatically, deploying real money would be premature.
